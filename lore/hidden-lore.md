# Hidden lore

> **Spoilers.** These truths are kept hidden in the novels (*Graceless Life*, *Bordering Life*, *Inquisitor's Descent*) but are defined here so the game can build on them. Public lore: [world.md](world.md). Characters: [characters.md](characters.md).

## The cosmos
- One **omnipotent force** disperses through everything and shapes existence into its many forms. Its will can't be measured or understood; the universe runs on it.
- Every world that takes shape borrows its matter from that force, and that is how the world keeps existing.
- **Deities** shape the worlds. There are many, all shaped by the omnipotent force. The world of Tzarot has several gods; **Ishdam is only one of them**, not the almighty god his Church preaches.
- **Universal law:** deities can't fight each other directly. The closest they can come to conflict is through their **creations** (e.g. Ishdam's Ishdarians against another deity's dragons at the opposite end of the world).
- A **world core** is a world's connection to existence. Souls pass through it to be cleansed of their will and desires and returned to the cycle of life and rebirth.

## Ishdam, the selfish god
- **Complete Ishdam** was full of pride and love, burning with devotion to their creator, the omnipotent force. Ishdam won the fight to shape the world's core; the **Father Tree** is that core.
- After the triumph, ambition took over: Ishdam wanted the **Ishdarians** to be greater than any other creation, mimicking their own "father". To do it, Ishdam broke a **universal taboo** and **split into three essences**:
  1. **The essence in the creation**: living in every Ishdarian. This is what Ishdam's love "loosened" and could never bind again (*Divine Restraints*: "Loved, and so set loose").
  2. **The loving protector**: on earth, in Ishdarian form.
  3. **The ruler**: keeping the divine role.
- It was a universal mistake. Split three ways, Ishdam lost their own self. The **ruler** grew prideful, shunned the loving protector, and used that divine force as leverage to **clog the world core**. That is the **Sentence of Sin**. The ruler has held control ever since.
- Records of Ishdam's virtue still exist, but today's depiction of Ishdam is mostly a tool of political and actual might.

## Why the Father Tree is ill
- The tree represents the world's connection to existence. With the loving essence lodged in the passage, souls can no longer pass through to be cleansed.
- The cycle of life and rebirth is stuck in **endless suffering**. The dead keep their will and desires, effectively immortal in a state of death: no breath, no heartbeat, only existence. It drives them insane.
- Souls with **weak wills** come back to the surface through the tree. That is what makes it ill: not decay, but a flood of greedy souls yearning for absolution. They try to take over living vessels to feel life again. The **Deceased**, the **Host**, Infestation and every other root-infested being come from this.
- The Sentence of Sin reached the whole world, because the core is the whole world's, not only Da'at Rav's (it also killed the great ravine at the opposite end).
- **Children are still born** because the loving Ishdam cleanses souls by itself, but it can't keep up with how many go through the passage of life and death.

## Pontiff Hans
- On the surface: a high official of Da'at Rav. The ranks technically put the Pontiff under the popes (who call themselves prophets), but he is the **real power**. He keeps the lower rank for freedom, politics and deception, so the world never learns Da'at Rav's weak spots.
- In truth: the **direct vessel of the selfish ruler Ishdam**, effectively the god himself.
- "Hansel", the Navat cult leader, borrows his name, and the two have connections.

## Danial
- Carries the **lingering essence of the loving Ishdam** (the reason he is "more than just strong" and still active three generations after his congregant days). He knows it.
- The loving Ishdam hopes, through Danial, to **end the Sentence**: to unclog the passage and give the role of cleansing souls back to their creator, the omnipotent force.
- In *Inquisitor's Descent* he is personally chosen not only by the loving Ishdam but by a **higher deity who governs time and fate**, who is helping Ishdam fix their mistake (since deities can't act against each other directly).
- The public prophecy calling Hans and Danial "the gateway of paradise" pairs the two surviving halves of one god.

## The Embracer
- The figure in black who destroyed the Apex Predator and made Klyne a "deal with the devil" (*Graceless Life*, Ch. 3) **is the Embracer**, known to the Navats as the **False Prophet**.
- One of the **world's wonders**. Kept deliberately mysterious in both the game and the novels: define nothing more about him without Lukas.
